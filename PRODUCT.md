# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

- **Lead audience for the brand: teachers, Model UN advisors and conference organizers** who choose Sessional for their school or circuit and pay for it. They need to trust it with students' data and with a conference that cannot fail on the day.
- **Students** run the conference: chairs run committees from a laptop, a student High Command (Secretariat) runs the event, and delegates follow on their phones. They are often minors (roughly 12–18). The brand must still feel like theirs.
- Schools in the United States and Mexico; everything ships in American English and Mexican Spanish.

## Product Purpose

Sessional runs Model United Nations conferences for schools. Chairs take roll call, run the speakers list and timer, handle motions, votes, working papers and resolutions from one console; the room's projector and delegates' phones follow the chair live; High Command sees every committee at once; grades, awards and certificates come out at the end. Success is a conference that runs on time, on the school's own branded site, without spreadsheets.

## Positioning

One product for the whole conference, from the chair's laptop to the Secretariat's overview, white-labelled per school (each school gets its own address, colors and words), built for conferences run by students with staff keeping the keys.

## Operating Context

- Used live in classrooms and school halls during a conference: a projector at the front, chairs on laptops, delegates on phones, often on unreliable Wi-Fi (the console keeps working offline).
- The rituals are Model UN's: roll call ("present", "present and voting"), placards raised to speak or vote, motions ranked by precedence, draft resolutions with numbered clauses, awards (Best Delegate, Outstanding Delegate, Honorable Mention, Best Position Paper).
- Rules of procedure vary: UN, Harvard, THIMUN, the Spanish reglamento, or a school's own.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Multi-tenant: each school's site carries its own theme, logo and colors; the Sessional brand appears on the product's own website, emails' fallback, the sign-in "Powered by Sessional" mark (removable on eligible plans) and the admin console.
- Plans: Starter, Conference, Institution, Circuit (organizations), with contracts on Institution and Circuit.
- Languages: English and Spanish, never labelled by dialect.
- Privacy: users are often minors; least data, no tracking, no third-party font or analytics requests from the product's pages.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **Sessional**, written lowercase in the current wordmark.
- **Keep the dot idea:** the current logo puts a dot over the "i" (amber on navy), and the app icon is that "i" and dot. The new identity keeps the dot as its recognizable mark; the wordmark, colors and icon may be redrawn from scratch (owner's decision, 2026-11-27).
- **No flags or national color schemes** in the logo or brand colors, so no country feels favored (owner's decision).
- The current palette (ink #14213D, paper #F4F1EA, amber #F2A541) is to be replaced: the owner considers it a default look (owner's decision, 2026-11-27).
- Website type is Zodiak (display) and Switzer (text) from Fontshare, IBM Plex Mono for figures (owner's decision). Fontshare fonts may be self-hosted for Sessional's own website and assets only, never offered to schools as a font choice, never committed to this public repository.
- Nothing is published until the owner approves a proposal.

## Product Principles

1. The chair's room comes first: anything shown must work on a projector, a laptop and a phone at once.
2. Students run it, staff keep the keys: credible to adults, respectful of students.
3. Every school is its own brand; Sessional's own brand stays quiet inside a school's site.
4. Least data: nothing that tracks or calls out to third parties from pages minors use.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA on every page (checked by axe in CI); high-contrast and larger-text display modes exist for every user; color is never the only signal.
